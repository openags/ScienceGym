# Directional radiative cooling: whole-paper robot task design

## Status and scope

This is an authored, source-grounded long-horizon task design for Bhatia et al., *Passive directional sub-ambient daytime radiative cooling*, Nature Communications 9, 5001 (2018), DOI [10.1038/s41467-018-07293-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07293-9). It is not a simulator, hardware integration, executable robot policy, fabricated dataset, or scientific replication. The complete main article was inspected through the publisher web reader; there is no locally verified main-article hash. The 25-page supplementary PDF was inspected locally, including figures 1, 2, 5 and 6. Publisher bytes are not exported.

The scope includes every identified physical experimental family, its material preparation and calibration, source-reported comparison conditions, acquisition, cleanup, and an honest record of unresolved inputs. Mathematical radiation models, COMSOL heat-transfer prediction, hypothetical spectral designs, and proposed cooler/photovoltaic applications are accounted for separately as nonmanual work. They do not become fabricated physical demonstrations.

The robot is a mobile, human-like operator. It retrieves labeled objects, carries supported assemblies between workstations, loads fixtures, attaches sensors, connects isolated instruments, sets readable controls, inspects feedback, moves the solar disk manually, and archives records. A chiller, spectrometer, sourcemeter/PID service and logger perform their own device processes after robot setup. The robot does not cause cooling by issuing an action name or receive future readings from the source paper.

## Scientific question and branch inventory

Can an emitter exchange radiation with the sky while being protected from direct sunlight, and how does observed temperature or balanced heater power depend on the specified assembly and conditions? Every empirical branch is retained:

1. Prepare coated copper emitters, two-layer cover, insulation/shield body, and a site/date-specific disk-reflector track; assemble identified white/black devices
2. Calibrate thermocouples against an immersion RTD in an isothermal block and retain channel-specific calibration provenance
3. Measure component direct-hemispherical optical reflectance in the solar and infrared ranges, for the reflector, both cover types and white/black emitters
4. Measure angle- and polarization-dependent emitter reflection and derive angular emissivity, retaining the angular branch separately from integrating-sphere measurements
5. Stage ambient, solar and weather instrumentation and synchronize records for every outdoor run
6. Acquire paired white/black stagnation time series with the movable disk reflector and nanoporous cover
7. Acquire paired cooling-power curves with attached, four-wire-connected heaters and PID temperature steps, then switch heaters off and observe recooling
8. Replace the disk/track arrangement with a fixed band reflector and replace the cover with two LDPE grocery-bag layers; acquire the paired nontracking stagnation run
9. Instrument a white-emitter device with six additional thermocouples and acquire three distinct thermal-map conditions: clear/scattered-cloud daytime, clear night, and hazy/cloudy near-noon

The branch list contains 11 selectable routes because the three thermal-map conditions are separate leaves. Preparation and environmental instrumentation are included routes, not implied magically completed preconditions. An episode selects a nonempty subset or a whole-paper work order. Completing one route does not complete the whole paper. The reference route is a dependency graph with loops and condition gates, not one mandatory action trace.

## Preparation, materials and identity

The source specifies a copper disk 50 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm thick; three coats of flat white or black Krylon Colormaster paint; two 25 mm FOAMULAR 150 insulation layers; a solid PE support tube with 76/102 mm inner/outer diameters; two nanoporous PE films, each 16 micrometres thick, on a 6.4 mm aluminum spacer ring with 107/127 mm inner/outer diameters; a 57 mm-tall polished aluminum cylinder with 140/152 mm inner/outer diameters; a 50 mm aperture; an acrylic base and aluminized Mylar lateral/base shielding. The movable reflector is a 60 mm polished aluminum disk. The source track was water-jet cut from 1.5 mm aluminum. These are reported dimensions, not a complete manufacturing drawing.

Coating, sharp-stock fabrication and water-jet processing are represented by qualified enclosed services or an explicitly declared prepared-component handoff. Inert classroom substitutes must have their own task-authored material and process cards. A substitute cannot silently inherit the paint's measured optical properties. The robot still issues stock, loads the supported service fixture, verifies the job and guard, waits for a safe-release receipt, unloads and inspects each part. Drying, surface preparation, coating thickness, adhesive details and dimensional tolerances remain input gates. No free spraying, cutting or machining procedure is invented.

Assembly is decomposed into base/frame attachment; two insulation layers; emitter installation with clear optical face; PE support; separate cover-film attachments to opposite spacer faces; side/base shielding; external cylinder/aperture alignment; and reflector-support installation. All joins, film faces, gap measurements and component identities are recorded. A fully painted emitter is not a prebuilt device. Wrinkled, torn or misplaced films are replaced with new component identities; replacement increments the assembly version and requires renewed inspection.

The textual reflector height is approximately 150 mm above the emitter, while SI Figure 1a2 labels a 100 mm vertical separation in its schematic. The drawing's datum is not sufficiently specified to reconcile these automatically. The task preserves this as a geometry ambiguity, requires a qualified working geometry, and never turns either number into verified CAD. Track or band shape depends on site, date, experiment window and selected height; a historical Cambridge track cannot automatically serve another location or day.

## Stations, movement and action boundaries

The authored scene has records, stock/carrier, enclosed fabrication, coating/service, assembly/inspection, calibration, UV-Vis-NIR sphere, FTIR sphere/angle fixture, outdoor acquisition, and archive/waste stations. These are symbolic contracts only. Missing robot kinematics, reachability, paths, gripper geometry, load limits, weather exposure limits and interlocks prevent physical execution claims.

Each move requires safe output state, disengaged station tethers, supported objects and a clear supplied route. The robot checks an empty destination dock, secures the carrier, retracts its arms, navigates, brakes, seats the carrier, and rechecks identity and integrity. It never teleports a sample between instrument records. Outdoor devices are transported by robust bases, not thin films or reflector tracks. Reaching across an illuminated aperture or moving a reflector can transiently shade the assembly: intervention times belong in the record and analysis masks.

The actor receives work-order goals, current inventory, qualified operating cards and observable instrument feedback. It does not receive source outcome targets, reference operation paths, hidden injected faults, evaluator checks or future data. Every proposed hidden fault must be observable or discoverable through available inspection/readback; secret knowledge cannot be necessary for success. An actor loader, scene and sensor implementations are not supplied.

## A readable end-to-end robot episode

A whole-paper work order spans multiple eligible sessions; it does not pretend all of the paper's historical weather conditions occurred on one day. The mobile robot begins at the records station, resolves the nonempty allocation, and drives to stock. It reads copper, insulation, support, film, reflector and coating labels, lifts each supported item into a retained carrier, and brings stock to the qualified fabrication or coating dock. It loads the fixture and uses the controls. The enclosed station processes the part; after explicit safe-release and cure feedback, the robot unloads and inspects it.

At the assembly bench, the robot physically builds each device from the identified layers: supports the base, fixes the frame, places both insulation disks, seats the painted emitter, places the PE support, stretches/attaches the two separately identified films to their spacer using the supplied qualified tools, fits shielding and aperture, then mounts the reflector geometry. It returns rejected parts to quarantine instead of editing a material label. It carries the temperature probes to calibration, seats them and the reference RTD in the block, runs the qualified bath program, withdraws the sensors only after safe release, and brings accepted probes back for attachment to the emitter rear faces.

For component optical work, the robot carries each identified component to the first instrument, loads its reference and holder, seats the component, starts and observes a scan, stops and unloads locally, and only then moves the retained component to the second instrument. Angular work uses a different fixture and current reference check, remounting each emitter and setting/readback-checking the scheduled angles and polarization states. Analysis is computed from the acquired records; the robot verifies their identities and processing cards.

For an eligible outdoor session, it wheels the isolated devices to the outdoor docks, seats their bases, attaches labeled leads and protected instruments, and verifies clocks and shadow geometry. It starts the covered baseline, physically lifts each aluminum lid into its holder, then returns periodically to the disk carriage to reposition it while the logger continuously measures. The operator's shadow and adjustment intervals are recorded. In the heater session it first returns the devices to the assembly bench for safe heater installation and four-wire connection, requalifies the current version, then at the outdoor controls runs the bounded plateau schedule and verifies output-off recooling.

For the alternate route it returns isolated devices to assembly, removes and archives the old reflector/films, installs the band and LDPE components and obtains a new-version inspection receipt before a nontracking run. For the three thermal-map sessions it attaches six extra calibrated probes according to the supplied location map and later runs only when each actual condition is eligible. Each session ends with acquisition commit, station-specific safe shutdown, supported unloading, recorded transport to archive, stock/waste reconciliation, and an honest complete, partial or aborted result. None of these actions is supplied as a demonstrated feasible robot trajectory.

## Calibration and optical routes

For temperature calibration, the operator seats identified thermocouples and the immersion RTD in the isothermal copper block, stages the block in the controlled bath, wires the reference meter and protected DAQ, starts the qualified bath schedule, and records synchronized readings after the supplied equilibration rule. The chiller establishes temperatures; the robot does not prescribe unreported calibration setpoints, sweep range, dwell, fit order or residual threshold. The source corrects individual thermocouple offsets and propagates slope error into measurement uncertainty. The still-missing fitting details, combination convention and acceptance criteria require a declared processing card and immutable raw observations. A calibration of one sensor does not apply to another or to a reconfigured channel without qualification.

Emitter thermocouples are attached near the rear center using the source-described thermally conducting silver paste. The source also describes the enclosed DAQ and cold-junction handling, a separate ambient RTD in a ventilated solar shield about five feet above the ground, and separate weather monitoring. Neither an exposed sun-heated probe nor the DAQ enclosure temperature can silently replace ambient air temperature.

At optical stations, the robot identifies the exact reflector, cover assembly or emitter, stages a clean supported mount, configures background/reference and measurement geometry, acquires and preserves scans, unloads and inspects. A representative coupon is allowed only through an explicit batch relationship; paper text does not establish historical serial identity across all experiments. The integrating-sphere branch and angle-resolved branch have separate holders, calibrations and records.

SI Figure 2 visibly labels incident angles 12, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 and 80 degrees. The angular route retains white and black emitters and s/p observations or an explicitly qualified polarization-averaging acquisition. Plotted symmetric polar display positions are not additional independent specimens or necessarily separate measured negative angles. The source does not give full scan settings, repeat counts, reference standards, alignment tolerances or the complete emissivity/transmittance derivation. In particular, transmission through PE cannot be equated to one minus reflectance without a justified absorption measurement/assumption. Optical processing must preserve its physical assumptions and available raw channels.

## Outdoor stagnation route

The operator stages the two devices adjacent to one another with separate channels, qualified orientation and nonoverlapping shadows. It mounts and checks the solar sensors, ambient shield and DAQ protection; records local time, UTC and site coordinates; verifies instrument clock alignment; and tests disk shadow coverage over the full emitter aperture. The source's weather logger sampled every five minutes. This is not evidence that emitter temperatures, PID or solar sensors were sampled at that rate; each acquisition cadence is a separate required input.

The source stagnation measurement covered the two hours before and after solar noon. Aluminum aperture lids were initially present and removed five minutes after acquisition began. The robot records actual lid identities and open times for both devices, then returns lids to assigned holders. Covered and open states are a transition/baseline comparison, not interchangeable with PE-cover removal or a full factorial shade control. The PE films remain installed.

During the disk-reflector run, the operator periodically repositions each reflector along its supplied track and verifies shadow coverage using observable geometry/inspection. The source's disk adjustment was manual; the separate automatic two-axis solar tracker belongs to the radiometry instruments. They are not the same mechanism. Reflector-motion frequency, alignment tolerance and sun-exposure abort thresholds are required task inputs. All interruption and transient-shadow intervals remain in the chronology.

Ambient temperature, DNI, sun-normal GTI, GHI, humidity, dew point and their sensor provenance accompany the temperature records where available. The source derives diffuse irradiance as GTI minus DNI for its co-aligned sun-normal instruments. Subtracting DNI from GHI is not the reported calculation. Invalid geometry, unsynchronized observations or significant negative differences require an invalid/uncertain diagnostic, not clipping to zero. Weather values are observations, not commanded conditions.

## Controlled-heater power route

At the assembly bench with output isolated, the operator installs the source-type Kapton heater beneath each emitter, keeps the temperature probe attached, establishes strain relief, and connects the four-wire circuit from the qualified port map. It logs an assembly revision because heater attachment and wiring alter thermal losses. Source agreement between heater-free and heater-bearing runs is not an equivalence assumption.

The sequence is heater-off passive cooling, verified stabilization, increasing approximately uniform temperature targets under PID control, steps extending above ambient if allowed by the qualified envelope, output-off, and verified return toward stagnation. The source reports five-minute steps and averaging the final two minutes of each step. The actual setpoint list, count, gains, sample cadence, power/current/temperature limits, stability threshold and emergency cutoff are not reported. Empty schedules cannot pass. The robot loads a qualified schedule, reads it back, arms only after wiring/sensor checks, and checks each dwell's timestamped feedback; the PID device provides the continuous control.

The final two-minute window remains anchored to the declared five-minute step. If it contains a fault, intervention or unsettled behavior, that step is incomplete; the operator cannot move the averaging window to nicer readings while claiming the source window. A deliberate extension or retry is a new labeled protocol variant/attempt, retaining the failed step. Each summary binds the temperature channel, electrical power channels, area, calibration versions, source accuracy term, fluctuations, condition signature and exact raw interval. Mean power divided by the measured emitter area is a steady-balance estimate only when stabilization and thermal-input assumptions hold. Transient input power is not cooling power.

The ambient-equal cooling-power estimate needs a declared interpolation or measurement rule and contemporaneous ambient data. Above-ambient points must not be labeled the source's ambient-equal maximum. Published performance numbers are evaluator-only context; a run with a different honest result can succeed procedurally. Figure-caption standard-deviation language and Methods instrument-accuracy/calibration contributions are both preserved; an authored uncertainty budget must state how they are combined rather than assume an unreported convention.

## Fixed band and LDPE route

This route changes two components together: a fixed 60 mm-wide band replaces the movable disk and two approximately 50 micrometre white LDPE grocery-bag layers replace the two nanoporous PE films. The robot disassembles the isolated supported devices, archives removed components, installs the qualified new parts, increments assembly versions, and verifies optical/shadow coverage. Separate matched devices are an alternative declared allocation, not inferred historical duplicates.

The band is fixed during the run but is site/date/window-specific. No within-run adjustment is counted as a successful nontracking run. If direct sunlight reaches the emitter, preserve the event and terminate or declare a separate modified attempt. White/black emitters are again observed contemporaneously. The source used another day from the tracked experiment; changing reflector, cover and weather means the comparison does not isolate the causal effect of tracking, reflector area or PE type. A full disk/band by nanoporous/LDPE factorial would be a task-authored extension, not a reported branch and is not silently added.

## Thermal-map validation route

A white-emitter device receives six additional location-specific thermocouples alongside its emitter sensor. SI Figure 5 shows seven temperature sites in each illustrated cross-section, but exact mounting coordinates and attachment recipes are not recoverable as manufacturing instructions from the plotted pixels. The operator must receive an explicit sensor-location map, attach each labeled sensor with the device isolated, inspect attachment/lead routing and bind every channel to a physical location. Guessing a coordinate from a schematic is insufficient.

The source reports three one-hour acquisitions under different conditions: daytime with scattered clouds about 1.5 hours after solar noon, clear night, and hazy/cloudy near-noon, across August 23-24, 2018. These are three distinct conditions, not three identical statistical repeats. The one-hour duration is the total map acquisition; baseline timing within that hour is a supplied schedule detail, not a reason to append an extra hour after a five-minute baseline. Each task route waits for or records the eligible actual weather/time condition and uses the provided equilibrium predicate before selecting its averaging interval. If no eligible conditions occur, the result is a valid blocked/partial route, not fabricated data.

The physical deliverable is a multichannel temperature map with coordinates, calibration, weather and averaging lineage. Comparing it with a supplied, versioned COMSOL prediction is optional separate analysis. A model heat-transfer coefficient, model wind condition or predicted map is never relabeled as a measured value. No COMSOL simulation is run or claimed by this package.

## Controls, repetitions and lineage

Source-supported comparisons include simultaneous white/black devices, initially closed/open apertures, heater-off/on/off chronology, seven-location thermal maps, and the combined fixed-band/LDPE alternative. Optical reference scans, electrical zero/output-off checks, repeat allocations, randomized station position and post-run drift checks are clearly task-authored quality controls unless separately evidenced. Their tolerances and frequencies require qualified input cards.

The source does not establish independent sample counts for a general statistical replication plan. Every work order declares specimen allocation, session count, technical repeats, failed-attempt retention and reuse rules. Two differently coated emitters are two conditions, not replicate n=2 for each condition. Seven sensors are locations, five-minute weather points are samples in time, and heater plateaus are operating points; none creates independent material replicates. Reused emitters retain serial identity and condition history. Changed coats, films, heaters, lead routes or sensor mounts create assembly/calibration revisions.

Every derived record traces to immutable raw records, physical sample/version, actual components, station fixture, calibration, software/program revision, clock basis, current location, environment record and inclusion/exclusion reasons. Derived data cannot be merged across incompatible condition signatures. Failed attempts remain visible, retries get fresh attempt IDs with predecessor links, and damaged components are quarantined. Models, source expectations, mock traces and episode measurements are distinct data classes.

## Cleanup, authored failure handling and completion

The operator stops acquisition only after committing records, disables heater output, verifies independent output-off feedback and safe temperatures, isolates leads, supports components before unclamping, places aperture lids for storage, secures reflectors/films, removes devices in retained carriers, returns usable stock and archives consumables or waste according to the supplied handling card. Sensor paste/coating residues are not wiped with an invented solvent. Failed-safe release causes an isolated station and intervention request, not a forced reach into a guarded fixture.

The authored recovery catalog includes swapped sensor channels, torn films, loose lead strain relief, wrong reflector path, clocks out of sync, dropped samples, lost shading, weather-window failure, PID instability, saturated electrical output, missing samples in the final window, and loss of a station's safe-release indication. Recovery preserves the failure, stops dependent output, uses observable diagnostics, requalifies repaired state, and starts a new attempt/version where appropriate. Weather cannot be repaired by editing the run label. A cooler ambient reading cannot be obtained by moving the reference probe into shade after the fact without recording a new setup.

Evaluation is independent of actor narration. It requires environment-side object/dock state, component and wiring graphs, interlock and power readbacks, raw acquisition receipts, synchronization and interval checks, then lineage-valid reports. Safe alternative action orders are accepted. Outcomes are complete, partial with valid blocker, safe abort, or failed, with scientific agreement reported separately. A valid missing-input stop is useful behavior but is not a completed experiment. Static checks in this package test authored schema/reference integrity and synthetic bookkeeping only. Robot feasibility, apparatus safety and scientific performance remain unverified.
